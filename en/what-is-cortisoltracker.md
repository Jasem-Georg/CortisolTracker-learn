CortisolTracker is a web app for people on corticosteroid replacement. It shows a calculated cortisol concentration in the blood for a person on that replacement, taking influencing factors into account. In short, a calculated cortisol profile for now and for the near future.

![CortisolTracker poster: a person's back, kidneys and adrenal glands](/learn/images/cortisol_ig_default.jpg)

## Who it is for

The app is for people whose doctor has already prescribed this replacement. That includes Addison's disease, secondary adrenal insufficiency, and congenital adrenal hyperplasia. Drugs in the log: hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, cortisone acetate, prednisolone, prednisone, and methylprednisolone.

![Space-filling model of the hydrocortisone molecule](/learn/images/hydrocortisone-molecule.png)

## Two modes

Dose calculation is the chart, the dose plan, and a look at how doses and factors add up into the curve. You use it when you are looking at the regimen and building the day. A computer or a tablet is easier to read.

Reminders is the phone screen for a day that is already built, marked as it happens. Around the Information window there is a cross of four swipes: left for doses, right for factors, up for the day plan, down for an extra dose.

![Five Reminders screens: schedule, taking a dose, information, factors, and an extra dose](/learn/images/reminders-screens.png)

The picture is one example, **NOT** a regimen to copy. The labels are English.

- Schedule — the next dose: time and amount.
- Time to take dose — buttons for the planned amounts. If the calculation is below the reference line, the window says by how much and points you to agree the dose with your doctor. The milligrams on the buttons are an interface hint, not a prescription.
- Information — the next dose, the calculated concentration, and the deviation from the target.
- Impact factors — mark what is acting this hour: coffee, food, stress, activity, and other factors from the list.
- Emergency dose — log a stress dose or an urgent intake. Signs of an adrenal crisis need medical help. This window does not replace that.

## What the chart shows in Dose calculation

The main screen of Dose calculation is the cortisol profile for the day. The curve is built from records, not from a blood or saliva test. It shows the concentration rising after a dose and then falling, and how factors shift it: food, coffee, stress, activity, a cold.

The chart is there so your day is easier to see, and so there is something to talk about with an endocrinologist: the shape of the curve next to how the day felt. The chart does not confirm that coverage is enough, and it does not recognise an adrenal crisis.

![Calculated day of 29 September 2026: three hydrocortisone doses, food, coffee, and activity](/learn/images/typical-day.png)

This is one ordinary day of one person, **NOT** a regimen to copy. The log has three hydrocortisone doses, food and coffee after the morning one, and activity later in the day. The blue line is the healthy reference profile of a person at rest. The purple line is the reference with factors: the same guide, shifted by those marks. Green, yellow, and red are the calculated concentration against that reference: green is normal, yellow is a moderate deviation, red is a critical deviation. The labels on the points are logged doses. The yellow dashed line is the current time; to the right of it the curves are dotted, which is the calculation for the remaining hours of the day. Under the chart, the same doses and factors are listed.

## What stays in the records

One day holds doses, influencing factors, and marks of how you feel: how the day feels, drowsiness, capacity to function, and a short note. Records can be kept in the account and opened from another device.

![State strip and the Current status panel with marks of how the day felt](/learn/images/day-records.png)

The picture is one example, **NOT** a regimen. On top is a strip of several days: doses, factors, and marks. Below is Current status and the form How you feel and notes. These marks do not enter the curve calculation. The labels are English.

When the calculated concentration falls, the app can show a hint for the next intake. That hint comes from your own records. The regimen still belongs to the doctor.

## Where CortisolTracker opens

CortisolTracker runs in the browser. The interface is in six languages: Russian, Ukrainian, English, German, Spanish, and French.

## What the app does not do

- It does not diagnose.
- It does not prescribe or change therapy.
- It does not replace sick-day rules, an emergency kit, or seeking medical help.
- It is not a medical device.
