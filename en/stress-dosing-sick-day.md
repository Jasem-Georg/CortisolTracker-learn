CortisolTracker is not a medical device and does not replace professional medical advice. Always consult your endocrinologist before changing your regimen.

This page is **one person’s logs**, not a sick-day protocol. Hydrocortisone milligrams, clock times and “how much extra” belong to a conversation with the care team. CortisolTracker did not decide the doses. It drew a **calculated** cortisol-equivalent picture of what was already logged.

## The patient

Male, 54. Secondary adrenal insufficiency caused by hypopituitarism. No meaningful endogenous cortisol. Replacement in these logs is **hydrocortisone** (the pharmaceutical name for cortisol). Other glucocorticoids exist in replacement; this case is not about them.

He has had two adrenal crises. Both were survived. Neither was guaranteed. The charts below are from September 2026.

## A day that was not an illness day

![Calculated hydrocortisone coverage on 10 September 2026, three daytime intakes, close to the ideal profile](/learn/images/stress-dosing-healthy-day.png)

**10 September 2026.** No acute illness, no unusual extra load.

On the chart:

- **Blue** — ideal daily profile (a healthy-style shape: higher in the morning, lower at night).
- **Green / yellow / red** — calculated coverage versus that profile (normal / moderate deviation / critical deviation).
- **Cyan dashed** — other medicines from the profile, informational only.

Three hydrocortisone intakes through the day sit on a curve that mostly follows the blue line: a morning rise, a smaller midday bump, then a decline. Yellow segments are deviation, not a diagnosis. The log is a well-managed ordinary day for this person — not a target for anyone else to copy.

## Two days later: acute respiratory illness

![Same person on 15 September 2026, illness factor overlay and larger evening intake](/learn/images/stress-dosing-sick-day.png)

**15 September 2026.** Fever, marked fatigue, systemic symptoms of a moderate-to-severe acute respiratory illness.

Same person, same app, different day. Three lines matter:

- **Blue** — the same healthy-style reference as before.
- **Purple** — the model’s **illness factor**: an elevated requirement while the infection is logged. A working adrenal cortex would raise output during significant illness; this patient’s glands are not doing that job.
- **Green / yellow / red** — calculated coverage from the hydrocortisone that was actually taken, including a larger intake late in the day.

The purple overlay is not a lab result and not “how much tablet to swallow.” It is the app’s factor curve. Fold-change slogans about cortisol in stress (often quoted as several times baseline) are physiology headlines, not a personal dosing table. Written sick-day rules — when to increase oral replacement, when to inject, when to go to hospital — come from the endocrinologist and from guidelines, not from a screenshot.

In this log the last intake is late and large. The stated reason was practical: overnight he cannot reliably watch himself, and he wanted modelled coverage through the night. Look at the picture honestly: that late dose also drives a **high evening peak** on the calculated curve (yellow then red). Extra overnight cover and a quiet evening floor are not the same thing. The chart makes that trade-off visible. It does not certify that the trade-off was “correct.”

## Why the two days are not the same feeling

A working adrenal axis can raise cortisol within minutes, then keep adjusting. Replacement is manual: anticipate, decide, act — using the sick-day plan you already have, pharmacokinetics, how this illness is unfolding, and what happened last time.

The gap between the two charts is not only “I feel worse.” It is the gap between an automatic endocrine response and a day that depends on tablets, timing and a plan agreed with a clinician. CortisolTracker does not close that gap. It can show logged coverage and logged factors on the same axes so the next clinic conversation has a picture instead of only a mood.

## What the app does not do here

- It does not diagnose infection or adrenal crisis.
- It does not prescribe stress-dosing, sick-day doubling, or an evening extra tablet.
- It does not replace an emergency injection kit or the instruction to seek urgent care.
- A red segment is a modelled deviation, not a proof of crisis and not a proof of safety.
