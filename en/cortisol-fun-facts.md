## Did you know that...

* The adrenal glands got their name due to their location, but are actually functionally unconnected to the kidneys in any way. They are completely independent endocrine glands.
* Cortisol levels in blood can increase 10 to 20-fold within minutes in response to acute stress or danger.
* Cortisol is involved in memory formation. A moderate increase in cortisol during learning improves memory consolidation, especially for emotionally significant events.
* The adrenal glands consist of two completely different parts: the cortex (produces cortisol) and the medulla (produces adrenaline). Essentially, they are two organs in one.
* You couldn't wake up in the morning without cortisol. The natural cortisol peak between 6-8 AM (cortisol awakening response) is the body's biological alarm clock.
* Astronauts on the ISS experience changes in cortisol's circadian rhythm due to the absence of the usual day-night cycle. They see a sunrise and sunset every 90 minutes.
* Cortisol is a natural anti-inflammatory agent. Synthetic analogs (prednisolone, dexamethasone) are used in medicine precisely because of this property.
* Cortisol levels can be measured not only in blood, but also in saliva, urine, hair, and even nails. Hair stores information about cortisol levels from recent months.
* Hugs actually lower cortisol levels. A 20-second hug with a close person triggers oxytocin release, which suppresses the cortisol response to stress.
* Chronic sleep deprivation (less than 6 hours per night) leads to a 50-100% increase in evening cortisol levels. The body perceives sleep deprivation as chronic stress.
* The embryo's adrenal glands begin cortisol production as early as the 8th-9th week of pregnancy, and it's precisely a sudden increase in fetal cortisol that triggers labor.
* People with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) often show paradoxically low basal cortisol levels. The adaptation system becomes exhausted.
